# Writing rules for member-facing output

- Write for a busy operator. Lead with the number and the action. One idea per sentence.
- Say "property owners", never bare "owners", in anything an owner might read.
- Every number carries its source and as-of date somewhere on the page. Never invent, estimate or round up a number. If a value is missing, write "not available" and why.
- Small samples read as directional: say "n=5, read directionally" rather than hiding the n.
- Recommendations to owners follow the client's rule table; Claude writes the sentence, the rule sets the stance. Flag anything the client must confirm with [CONFIRM].
- Do not use em dashes. Use a period or a comma.
- Avoid the reflexes that read as machine-written: "Here's the thing", "It's not X, it's Y", "shift" as an abstract move, sentences starting with "Most", "I've seen this play out", stacked identical openers, three-item fragment runs, and setup questions like "The result?".
- Talk tracks are spoken language: contractions, short lines, one ask.
- Confidential: survey answers and comments stay on internal pages. Owner-facing copy may thank an owner for feedback but never quotes their words or cites their score.
